01

A safe place to discover.

Chipo enters the science room. A bag blocks the doorway and a glass beaker is cracked. What should she do before the activity starts?

Starter · Think first

What you will learn

  • Give a reason for a safety rule.
  • Identify hazards and choose precautions.
  • Identify protection and use it as shown.
  • Practise the school’s emergency response.
  • Communicate a clear safety message.

What you need

  • A notebook and pencil.
  • Paper and colours for your poster.
  • A partner, if one is available.
  • A teacher for equipment and first-aid practice.
Laboratory

A place for practical science work.

Hazard

Something that could cause harm.

Precaution

An action that reduces the chance of harm.

Safety protocol means an agreed set of steps for doing something safely. Everyone in the room shares responsibility for following it.

Our goal: safe actions, with reasons. Reading notes is the beginning; practical skills need supervised practice.
02

Small habits. A big difference.

Before practical work

  • Wait for the teacher’s instructions.
  • Read the task before starting.
  • Keep bags out of walkways.
  • Tie back long hair. Secure loose clothing.
  • Wear closed shoes and the required protection.
  • Report damaged apparatus before use.

During practical work

  • Walk. Do not run, push or play roughly.
  • Use apparatus only as instructed.
  • Do not eat, drink or taste substances.
  • Do not smell an unknown substance directly.
  • Keep your hands away from your face.
  • Tell the teacher about spills or injuries at once.

After practical work

  • Follow instructions for stopping and putting away equipment.
  • Leave your work area clear.
  • Use the correct waste collection. Ask if unsure.
  • Wash your hands.

A rule needs a reason.

A common mistake: “Goggles mean I can do anything.”

Protection reduces some risks. You must still follow instructions, use suitable apparatus and avoid unsafe actions.

Explain it · Notebook task

Write two rules. Give a different reason for each one.

Example: “Tie back long hair so it does not catch in equipment or reach a heat source.”

03

Look closer. What could go wrong?

Explore this practice picture. For each numbered hazard, explain the possible harm and a safer action.

Explore · Tap a number
Choose a number to explore the picture.

Use pictures for practice. Do not make real hazards.

Try these other situations

An unlabelled bottleDo not open, taste or use it. Tell the teacher so it can be identified and handled safely.
A hot objectYou may not see that it is hot. Follow the teacher’s handling instructions and use the right equipment.
A damaged electrical leadKeep away. Tell the teacher before the equipment is used.
Hazard → possible harm → precaution
A bag in a doorway → someone could trip → store bags in the teacher’s chosen place.

Classroom investigation

  1. With your teacher, look around the room along a safe route.
  2. Record three hazards or use three picture cards.
  3. For each, describe the possible harm and a precaution.
  4. Share one finding. Listen to another group’s idea.

Observe only. Do not touch or move dangerous items. At home, use the picture above instead of inspecting equipment.

04

Choose protection with a purpose.

PPE stands for personal protective equipment. Choose it for the hazard and the activity, with your teacher’s guidance.

Goggles

Protect the eyes. Wear the correct type and fit them as shown.

Laboratory coat

Protect the body and clothes. Fasten it and secure loose sleeves.

Closed shoes

Cover the feet and toes. Keep footwear secure.

Gloves: different materials protect against different hazards. Use only the type approved for the task. A face shield, if required, must be used as instructed alongside other suitable protection.

Apply · Match the protection

Show it, then explain it

  1. Watch your teacher demonstrate suitable PPE.
  2. Check it for damage with the teacher.
  3. Put it on and take it off as shown.
  4. Explain what it protects and one limitation.

If equipment is unavailable, identify it using pictures. Ask your teacher to arrange the practical demonstration later; pictures alone cannot show correct handling.

05

Stay calm. Know what to do.

Before practical work, know your school’s alert signal, exit route, meeting point and who provides first aid.

01 · Stop

Stop your own activity when it is safe to do so.

02 · Keep clear

Avoid the danger and warn others nearby.

03 · Get help

Tell the teacher what happened and where.

Decide · What would you do?

A device starts sparking.

If you see a spill

  • Keep away and warn others.
  • Tell the teacher what and where.
  • Do not touch or clean an unknown spill.

If there is a fire

  • Alert the teacher and others.
  • Follow the school’s evacuation procedure.
  • Go to the meeting point for a head count.
  • Do not fight the fire or return for belongings.

Safety equipment: know the purpose

Item or placePurposeYour role
First-aid kitSupplies for immediate careKnow its location; get a trained adult.
Eye-wash or safety showerEmergency rinsing equipmentLocate it and follow the teacher’s demonstrated procedure.
Fire alarm and exitAlert people and provide a way outKnow the signal and practise the approved route.
Fire extinguisherFire control by a trained personKnow its location. Do not operate it independently.

First aid is a practical skill

First aid is the immediate help given to someone who is injured or suddenly ill. Get a trained adult immediately. Do not put yourself in danger or touch someone who may still be in contact with electricity.

Teacher-led practical · Required for the safety sequence

Station A — report a hazard: use a picture of broken glass. One learner keeps others clear; another reports the location to the teacher. Swap roles.

Station B — practise an evacuation: the teacher explains the school procedure, leads a calm drill and checks everyone at the meeting point.

Station C — first-aid demonstration: a trained first aider demonstrates an appropriate basic response using safe training materials. Learners practise only the demonstrated actions, under supervision. The teacher observes, gives feedback and repeats practice where needed.

No real fire, chemicals, live wires or injuries are used. First-aid technique must come from the school’s approved training, not from guessing on this page.

Reflect with a partner

  • Which action was easy to remember?
  • Which action needs more practice?
  • How did your group help everyone stay safe?
06

Make a message that helps someone.

A useful safety poster has one accurate message, a clear picture and a reason people can understand.

Create · Your safety poster
◉—◉Protect your eyes.

Wear the goggles your teacher recommends for the activity.

Share, review, improve

  1. Show your poster to a partner.
  2. Ask them to explain its message without your help.
  3. Check the safety advice with your teacher.
  4. Improve any unclear words or pictures.

Prefer paper? Draw a large picture, add your message and explain your choice to the class.

07

What will you do differently?

Try these original practice questions. Then explain one safety choice in your own words.

Written practice · 6 marks

  1. Describe a hazard, the harm it could cause and a suitable precaution. [3]
  2. Name one item of PPE and explain what it protects. [2]
  3. State who you should alert when an accident happens. [1]
Show model answers and marking guidance

1. A cable across a walkway [1] could trip someone [1]. Keep clear and ask the teacher to have it secured safely [1]. Accept other correct examples.

2. Suitable goggles [1] protect the eyes from the relevant hazard [1]. Accept other correct item-and-purpose pairs.

3. Alert the teacher or laboratory supervisor [1].

These are author-created study questions, not an official ECZ examination or marking scheme.
