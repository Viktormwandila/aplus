1 / Look around

What is Physics?

Physics is the study of matter, energy and how things work.

Matter means “stuff”.

Matter has mass and takes up space. Water, air, a stone and your school bag are matter.

Mass tells us how much matter an object has.

Energy helps things happen.

Energy can be transferred when something moves, heats up or gives out light.

Sunlight gives energy to a solar panel. A battery can supply energy to a torch.

You already see Physics every day.

A bicycle

Push the pedals and the bicycle moves. The brakes use friction to slow it down.

A solar panel

It changes energy from sunlight into electrical energy.

A drum

The drum skin vibrates. These vibrations make sound.

New word: friction. A force that opposes movement between surfaces touching each other.
Different parts of Physics

Mechanics: movement and forces, like a moving bicycle.
Thermal physics: heat and temperature, like warming water.
Waves: sound and light.
Electricity and magnetism: circuits, batteries and magnets.
Atomic physics: the tiny parts that make up matter.
Electronics: circuits used in radios and phones.
Geophysics: physical processes in the Earth.

2 / Work safely

First, make the laboratory safe.

A laboratory is a place where we do practical investigations.

Look for three hazards. A hazard is something that may cause harm.

Remember these five rules.

  1. Listen to your teacher.
  2. Walk. Do not run or push.
  3. Wear the protection needed for the activity.
  4. Keep your work area clear.
  5. Report damage and injuries at once.
If something goes wrong: stop work, move away from danger and tell the teacher. Follow the school’s emergency instructions.

Know where the exit and first-aid box are. A trained adult should give first aid. Do not touch exposed wires or try to repair electrical equipment.

3 / Care for our space

Where should waste go?

Waste is material we no longer need. Different waste needs different care.

Clean paper

Use both sides when possible. Reuse clean paper or put it in the school’s paper collection.

Broken glass

Keep away and tell the teacher. It needs a safe container for sharp waste.

Used batteries

Give them to the teacher for a suitable battery collection. Do not burn them.

Reduce → Reuse → Recycle. Use less, use suitable materials again, and send recyclable waste to the correct collection.

Help Chipo sort the waste.

Where should it go?

Follow the waste system at your school. Never pour an unknown liquid into a sink.

4 / Meet the tools

Choose the right apparatus.

Apparatus means the tools we use in practical work. Tap each tool.

No special tools? A clean marked bottle can help compare liquid amounts, but it may not measure volume accurately. Ask the teacher before making or using any improvised apparatus.

5 / Think like a scientist

Ask. Try. Look. Explain.

An investigation is a careful way to answer a question using evidence.

Chanda’s question: “Will the same toy car move faster down a steeper ramp?”

1Ask a clear question.
2Make a prediction.
3Test and record.
4Use the evidence.

Make it a fair test.

Change only the ramp angle. Use the same car, the same ramp surface and the same distance along the ramp. Release the car from rest without pushing.

Repeat each test. Compare the times.

Explore a simple model.

Gentle · 5°Steep · 35°

Modelled travel time: 0.89 s

About this model · optional

This model treats the car as a particle sliding from rest with no friction or air resistance: time = √(2d / (g sin θ)), d = 1 m and g = 9.81 m/s². A real wheeled car behaves differently. You do not need this formula for Topic 1.1. Use the model to notice a pattern.

What should a short science report include?

Your question, prediction, method, results and conclusion. Tell us what the evidence shows. A prediction can be wrong; honest results still help us learn.

6 / Check what you know

You’ve learnt a lot. Let’s try!

5 short questions · 5 marks. Original practice questions, not an official examination.

A question to explain in your own words

Give one example of Physics in your home or community. Say what happens and which idea it shows.

Example: “Our bicycle brakes slow the wheel. This shows friction.” Other correct examples are welcome.
