1 / Get ready

Laboratory safety

How can we learn together without putting anyone in danger?

Three words to know

  • Laboratory: a place for practical science work.
  • Hazard: something that could cause harm.
  • Precaution: an action that helps prevent harm.

By the end, try to:

  • Spot an unsafe situation.
  • Choose a safer action and give a reason.
  • Show one safety rule in a role play.
  • Identify suitable protection.
  • Show how to get help.
What is Physics? Physics helps us study matter, energy, forces and motion. Practical work helps us test ideas. Safe habits come first.

Think and say

  • Look at your classroom or laboratory.
  • Name one thing that could hurt someone.
  • Tell a partner how you could prevent it.

Use a picture or an imagined example if you are studying at home. Do not search for or touch dangerous objects.

2 / Short study notes

Simple rules. Safer science.

Before practical work

  • Wait for the teacher’s instructions.
  • Read the task before starting.
  • Keep bags out of walkways.
  • Tie back long hair. Secure loose clothing.
  • Wear closed shoes and the required protection.
  • Report damaged apparatus before use.

During practical work

  • Walk. Do not run, push or play roughly.
  • Use apparatus only as instructed.
  • Do not eat, drink or taste substances.
  • Do not smell an unknown substance directly.
  • Keep your hands away from your face.
  • Tell the teacher about spills or injuries at once.

After practical work

  • Follow instructions for stopping and putting away equipment.
  • Leave your work area clear.
  • Use the correct waste collection. Ask if unsure.
  • Wash your hands.

A rule needs a reason.

3 / Look closely

What could cause harm?

A practice picture. Never create real hazards for this activity.

Use this sentence:
“I can see ____. It could cause ____. A safer action is ____.”

Now explain another situation.

  • A bottle has no label. What should you do?
  • Answer: do not use it. Tell the teacher so it can be identified and handled safely.
4 / Protect yourself

Dress for safe science.

PPE means personal protective equipment. It helps protect your body. It does not make an unsafe action safe.

Goggles → eyes

  • Use the correct goggles for the task.
  • Wear them as the teacher shows you.

Coat → body and clothes

  • Fasten it properly.
  • Keep loose sleeves away from apparatus.

Closed shoes → feet

  • Cover the toes and feet.
  • Keep footwear secure.
Gloves: use the type approved for the activity. Different gloves protect against different hazards. Ask the teacher before choosing them.

Quick choice

An activity may splash liquid towards your face. Which item helps protect your eyes?

5 / Practise together

Stop. Keep clear. Get help.

If you see danger, act calmly and tell the teacher.

Know your room

  • Locate the exit and the meeting point.
  • Locate the first-aid box.
  • Know how to alert the teacher.
  • Follow the school’s emergency procedure.

Know your limits

  • Do not clean up an unknown spill.
  • Do not touch exposed wires or sparking equipment.
  • Do not touch someone who may still be in contact with electricity.
  • Do not fight a fire. Alert an adult and follow evacuation instructions.
First aid is immediate help for an injured or suddenly ill person. Get a trained adult. Practise only the actions your teacher has safely demonstrated and authorised.

Two-minute role play · no real danger

  1. One learner says, “There is broken glass near the bench.”
  2. A second learner warns others and moves them clear.
  3. A third learner tells the teacher where the hazard is.
  4. Swap roles. Explain why each action matters.

Use a drawing or card instead of glass. At home, describe the actions aloud to a responsible adult.

Make one safety message

  • Choose one rule.
  • Draw a clear picture.
  • Add a short message, such as “Keep the exit clear”.
  • Explain how your message helps others.
6 / Have a go

Can you make the safer choice?

Five original practice questions. Your teacher also needs to see you practise safe behaviour.

Show what you can do

  • Identify a hazard in a picture.
  • Explain a suitable precaution.
  • Show how to wear available PPE correctly.
  • Role-play reporting a hazard clearly.

An online score checks your answers. It does not certify practical competence.

Short-answer practice · model answer

Question: State a laboratory rule and explain its importance.

Example: “Keep bags out of walkways. This helps prevent trips and falls.” Other suitable rules with correct reasons are welcome.

Read it. Explain it. Practise it.
Lesson 1 · Safety rules (Laboratory safety)

Original teaching adaptation aligned to CDC Physics Syllabus (2024), Form 1, 1.1.1.1, printed p. 2. Informed by CDC Physics Teaching Module, Form 1 Term 1 (2025), introduction and safety guidance. This is a proposed first lesson, not an official word-for-word script. Teacher review pending.

For the teacher · scope, plan and sources

Suggested 40-minute plan: starter and vocabulary 5 min; rules and reasons 8 min; picture hazards 8 min; PPE demonstration 7 min; role play 7 min; exit questions 5 min. Adapt to your learners and resources. Poster work can continue next lesson.

Observation: record each practical skill as independent, with support, or not yet demonstrated. Give feedback and another chance to practise. Use pictures, verbal descriptions and accessible routes to include all learners. Never stage real spills, fire, broken glass or live electrical hazards.

Scope: this lesson starts safety practice. Supervised emergency drills, first-aid instruction and further safety work need follow-up. Waste management, apparatus, fundamental concepts and applications are later subtopics; the whole introduction is not one period.
