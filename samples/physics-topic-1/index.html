01 / Observe

A scientist starts with safety.

Interactive lab

Look closely. Select each numbered marker to discover a hazard and the safer action.

Your first lab habit

Pause. Check. Tell.

Before touching apparatus, listen to your teacher and check the workspace. Wear the protective equipment required for the activity.

Pause: stop work when something seems unsafe.
Check: keep a safe distance and protect others from the hazard.
Tell: report spills, damaged equipment and injuries to the teacher immediately.

Know the exit, first-aid location and emergency procedure. Follow trained adult instructions; do not handle broken glass or attempt an electrical repair.

03 / Investigate

From “I wonder” to evidence.

Mini investigation

Physics explores matter, energy and how they interact. Its branches include mechanics, thermal physics, waves, electricity and magnetism. A good investigation starts with a question we can test.

Does a longer pendulum swing more slowly?

Imagine a small mass swinging from a string. Change only the string length, and compare the time for one complete swing.

Change: string length.
Measure: time for one complete swing (the period).
Keep the same: mass, small release angle and location.

In a supervised practical, time 10 complete swings and divide by 10. Repeat and compare measurements. Never swing a mass near someone.

How do I report an investigation?

State your question and prediction. Describe the method and safety precautions. Record measurements with units, look for a pattern and explain what the evidence supports. Mention uncertainty and how the investigation could improve. A result that disagrees with your prediction is still useful evidence.

04 / Make connections

Good science cares for its surroundings.

Reduce & reuse

Clean paper and packaging

Use only what you need. Reuse clean materials where suitable and place recyclable waste in the school’s designated collection.

Separate safely

Glass, batteries and electronics

Tell the teacher about broken glass. Batteries and electronic waste need designated collection; do not burn them or mix them with ordinary litter.

Ask before disposal

Unknown liquids and materials

Never pour an unknown substance down a sink. Your teacher will identify the waste and arrange appropriate disposal.

Physics is already part of your day.

A bicycle brake uses friction to slow a wheel. A solar panel converts sunlight into electrical energy. A phone speaker turns electrical signals into vibrations that produce sound.

Reveal marking guide · self-marked

Award one mark each for: changing string length; measuring time per complete swing (or timing several swings and dividing); keeping mass or a small release angle constant; and a valid precaution such as securing the support or keeping the swing area clear.
